Rain Graph & Bars Guide

The Rain card shows up to two charts side by side: Next 2 hours (15-minute bars) when 15-minute data is available for the place, and Next 24 hours (hourly bars and a chance curve), always. Each chart's legend names its series.

Bars = rain rate (how hard), left axis
The blue bars show the expected precipitation rate in in/h (Imperial) or mm/h (Metric). Read them against the left axis: the top label (Rate · 0.1 in/h or Rate · 2.5 mm/h), the middle line (half of it) and 0. The scale starts at 0.1 in/h (2.5 mm/h) and doubles until the largest bar fits, so light rain still shows. On the 2-hour chart each bar is the 15-minute amount expressed as an hourly rate.
Curve = rain chance, right axis
On the 24-hour chart the amber curve shows the chance of measurable precipitation (at least 0.1 mm, about 0.004 in) in each hour, read against the right axis (100%, 50%, 0%).

Callouts and hover

  • Peak bars: the two highest bars carry their rate (for example 0.08 in/h).
  • Peak chance: the highest point of the curve is labelled (for example Peak 80%).
  • Hover over any slot for a guide line and a readout such as 3 PM · Bar: 0.06 in/h · Chance: 70%; slots without a time label read +2h or +30m.
  • The time axis shows up to 5 labels, starting with Now. A chart without data says No data.
  • VoiceOver reads a summary of each chart, for example Highest chance: 70% at 3 PM.

How heavy is it?

These are the bands the Rain card's sentences use.

Words usedMetric rateImperial rateWhat it is like
Very lightbelow 0.5 mm/hbelow 0.02 in/hDrizzle or scattered drops; surfaces barely wet.
Light0.5 – 2.5 mm/h0.02 – 0.10 in/hSteady light rain; puddles form slowly.
Moderate2.5 – 7.6 mm/h0.10 – 0.30 in/hPuddles form quickly; spray off roofs and roads.
Heavy7.6 mm/h and more0.30 in/h and moreSheets of rain, fast runoff, poor visibility.

Alaska, Hawaii and the rest of the worldNone: only the 24-hour chart is shown

The quarters are rescaled so that each hour's four quarters add up to the blended hourly amount, so the two charts never disagree about how much falls in an hour.
