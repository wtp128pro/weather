Adding & Managing Places

You can save up to 50 places. The list, its order, the names you give and the selected place are kept between launches.

Adding a place

Press ⌘N, click + in the toolbar, or (when the list is empty) click Add a city. The Add a city window opens.

  1. Type what you are looking for in Address, Landmark, or City: a city ("Lyon"), a street address ("1600 Pennsylvania Ave NW, Washington"), a ZIP or postal code, a landmark, or coordinates such as 25.76, -80.19, 25.76 -80.19 or 25.76°, -80.19°. At least 2 characters are needed. With an empty field the window suggests a few cities.
  2. Optionally give it a name in Location Name: type one, or click a preset (Home, Work, Friend's place, Family, School, Gym, Vacation; click again to clear it). The name replaces the place name in the sidebar and in the forecast header, and picks a matching symbol (a house for Home, a briefcase for Work, and so on).
  3. Check the units in the units menu, labelled Units for and the name (with its state or province) of the result that would be added (Units while there is no result): Imperial (°F, mph, in) or Metric (°C, km/h, mm). Until you pick one yourself it follows the result that would be added, by its country: Imperial for the United States and its territories, Liberia and Myanmar, Metric elsewhere, and the default units for new places when the country is not known. Once you pick, your pick is saved with every place this window adds. Each place keeps its own units.
  4. Add it: double-click a result, press Return or Space on the highlighted result, or click its Add button. Return in the name field adds the highlighted (or first) result. A place you already saved shows Added.

The window closes after a successful add, and the new place is selected. Esc closes the window without adding. There is no map to pick a point on: to save an exact spot, type its coordinates.

With an empty list, the first page suggests a few cities to add with one click. Its units menu works the same way for them: it is labelled Units for and the city under the pointer or keyboard focus (the first city when neither is on one), and follows that city's country until you pick. A pick there is not carried into the Add a city window.

How the search works

  • Two searches run side by side as you type: a weather-location search of cities (Open-Meteo, up to 10 results), and an address search that asks Apple Maps, OpenStreetMap Nominatim and Photon, plus the US Census geocoder for text that starts with a house number.
  • When the address search finds anything, its results are shown (up to 20, duplicates merged: the same title within 150 m, or the same city within 5 km). Otherwise the city results are shown.
  • Typed coordinates are named by a reverse lookup; when that fails the result is titled with the coordinates and described as Exact coordinates.
  • The status line tells you what is happening: Searching addresses and weather locations…, Matching addresses & locations (N), Type at least 2 letters, No results, or Search failed: ….

The sidebar

Each row shows the condition icon, the name (or your Location Name), the region ("State, Country"), a rain hint, and the temperature with its unit in the place's own units (for example 77°F beside a place in 18°C). The rain hint is the first of: Raining until 3:30 PM, Raining now, Rain in 25 min, Rain starting now, Rain at 4 PM, Rain tomorrow, 4 PM, Rain Friday, 4 PM, Rain expected, Showers possible, Dry for 48 h; or Loading…, Can't load forecast, Offline, No data yet, No rain data. When a nearby station reports precipitation while the forecast says dry, the hint says so (for example Light rain now).

An orange pill marks a row whose forecast is Offline, Stale (the last refresh failed) or Updating…; offline and stale rows are dimmed. Hover over a row for the full place name.

A warning triangle after the name marks a place with an official alert in force. Its colour is the highest level among them (red, orange, yellow; light blue for minor, white for an unknown level), and a number beside it counts the alerts when there is more than one. VoiceOver reads the alerts with the row, for example "Alert: Tornado Warning (red)". See Weather Alerts.

Selecting, reordering and removing

  • Select a row with a click or the arrow keys. The selection is remembered.
  • Reorder: drag a row onto another row; a blue line shows where it will go. Or use Move up / Move down in the row's … menu.
  • Remove: … › Remove. There is no confirmation and no undo; the place's cached forecast and its learned local corrections are forgotten with it.

Naming a place

Use … › Set Location Name for a preset (and Clear Custom Name), or … › Name / Edit Location… (also the Name Location / Edit Name button in the forecast header) for the Name & Address Details sheet:

  • Location Name: your name for the place, with the same presets.
  • Street Address / Locality Subtitle: the line shown under the name.
  • The coordinates are shown for reference; they do not change. Save (or Return) keeps the forecast and the list position; Clear Name removes the custom name.
