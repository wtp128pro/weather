Live Station Observations

To capture local microclimates (sea breezes, valley cold pools, urban heat islands, and local gust fronts), Neginato Mezora reads every station network that covers the place at each refresh, tests the stations against each other, and blends the trusted ones, with the same algorithm in every country. The result is mixed with a high-resolution model analysis for the exact spot (90 m terrain) to give the forecast page's "now".

National networks are searched a little beyond their borders, so places near a border also get the neighbouring country's stations. Every network can be turned off in Settings › Stations.

Each network starts from a trust level for its class of station: national and WMO-standard stations count fully; partner, road-weather and hydrological stations less (for example MET Norway's partner stations 70% and Météo-France's extended network 85%), and their wind much less, because many measure it low or in sheltered spots.

2. 3D anisotropic spatial kernel

Each station at horizontal distance d (km) and elevation difference Δz (m) is weighted by:

W_horiz(d) = exp(-d² / (2 * 22.0²)) / (1 + (d / 8.0)²)
W_vert(Δz) = exp(-Δz² / (2 * 350.0²))
W          = W_horiz(d) * W_vert(Δz) * trust * health

The 12 stations of largest weight within 60 km are blended. Reports less than 1.5 km apart (an airport's METAR and its national twin) count as one station.

3. Diurnal lapse-rate elevation reduction

A station's temperature is moved to the place's elevation with a lapse rate that depends on the time of day:

  • Daytime: Γ_T = -0.0068 °C/m (6.8 °C/km, a mixed boundary layer)
  • Night-time: Γ_T = -0.0042 °C/m (4.2 °C/km, damped by inversions)
  • Dew point: Γ_Td = -0.0019 °C/m

Station elevations come from the station lists or, for networks that give none, from the Open-Meteo elevation API.

4. De-clustering, freshness and outlier control

  • Angular de-clustering (Shepard, 1968): stations clustered along the same compass bearing from the place share an angular shadow penalty W_dir = 1 / (1 + shadow), so one dense cluster cannot outvote the other directions.
  • Freshness: weights decay with the age of the report, with time constants of 45 minutes for temperature, 20 minutes for wind and 15 minutes for the weather.
  • Wind direction: averaged as a vector, never as a number of degrees: each station adds its speed in the direction the wind comes from, calm stations count as zero, and stations that report no direction are left out. No direction is given when the result is under 1 km/h or under a quarter of the mean speed (winds too spread or opposed); the "now" card then uses Open-Meteo's current direction, or draws no arrow.
  • Outlier control (Rousseeuw & Croux, 1993): when at least 3 stations report, a sensor more than 3σ from the median (σ = 1.4826 × MAD, at least 1.5 °C) is down-weighted by (3σ / r)², so real contrasts of up to 4.5 °C are never penalised.
  • Short-range bias decay (Glahn & Lowry, 1972): the observed temperature difference from the forecast fades out over the first 6 forecast hours: ΔT(t) = ΔT_0 * exp(-Δt / 3.5h).

5. Stations are tested continuously

At every refresh each station is compared with what its neighbours predict for it. Its track record (bias by day and by night, and scatter) sets its health: full weight while its error stays within 1.5 °C, less beyond that, none from 8 °C; a sensor whose reading has not changed for 8 hours is set aside. New stations, and stations that come back, start on probation. A station that stops reporting for 3 days of app use is dropped. The catalog of stations is kept on disk, so restarts lose nothing; time with the app closed never counts. Settings › Stations shows each nearby station's weight and why.

6. The forecast learns from the stations

When close, fresh stations are available (within about 25 km and 45 minutes), the app learns how far the models run from them for each place and hour of the day, and corrects the forecast temperatures by up to ±4 °C. See Learned Local Corrections.
