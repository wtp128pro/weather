Weather Alerts

Neginato Mezora shows the official weather warnings in force for each saved place, as the issuer published them. The app does not make up, forecast or re-rate alerts, and it does not send notifications: alerts are shown only in the window, on the forecast page and in the sidebar. No alert source needs a key or an account, and there is no setting for them.

Where alerts come from

Each place is served by exactly one source, chosen by its country:

Places in any other country read Alerts not available.

The Alerts card

On the forecast page, below Now, each alert in force gets a card, highest level first (then the earliest start):

  • The event name (for example Tornado Warning) and a badge with a warning triangle and its level: Red, Orange, Yellow, Minor or Unknown, drawn in the level's colour on a dark background. VoiceOver reads this line as the card's heading. The level is taken from the colour or severity the issuer gives.
  • The issuer's headline, then Effective Today 3:00 PM and Expires Friday 6:00 AM, in the place's time zone, when the issuer gives them.
  • The issuer's instructions, if any, and Details, which expands the full description.
  • Source: National Weather Service (or the other issuer), linked to the issuer's page for the alert when it gives one.

In the sidebar, a warning triangle after the place's name, coloured by the highest level, with the number of alerts when there is more than one. Both disappear when the alert expires or ends, without a new download.

Last checked: Today 3:00 PM.The newest answer is older than its source's window (30 minutes for MeteoAlarm, 10 minutes for the others), for example while offline or while the window is hidden. A Source: … line under the text names the issuer the place is routed to. A failed check does not hide alerts from an answer still within its window.

How often alerts are checked

  • A place's alerts are checked only after one of its forecast refreshes completes (with a forecast or an error), never on their own timer and never while offline. They therefore follow the forecast refresh: while the window is visible, about every 10 minutes, and with ⌘R.
  • MeteoAlarm: a country's feed is downloaded at most once every 30 minutes, shared by every place in that country. A failed download is reused for 120 seconds, so the places of one country refreshed within that time cost one attempt; a later refresh tries again. The feed may be up to 16 MiB.
  • NWS, Bright Sky, MET Norway, Environment Canada: an answer for a point is reused for 5 minutes, also by another place at the same point; a failed one is not reused. As its terms require, a MET Norway answer is reused until the expiry time it sends, and then asked for again with If-Modified-Since.
  • Shown alerts are checked against the clock: one that expires or ends disappears at that time (after the Mac wakes from sleep, within a minute), without a new download.

Limitations

  • Alert areas follow each issuer's published region shapes, which can differ from local boundaries near borders and coasts. In Ireland, MeteoAlarm's own county outlines put a few border towns in a neighbouring county: the centre of Waterford city lies in County Kilkenny's outline, and Youghal, Carrick-on-Shannon and Killaloe likewise lie in a neighbouring county's. A warning for their own county may not reach them, while a neighbour's does.
  • Coastal places and islands outside the land outlines (for example Howth, Bray, Greystones, Malahide, Tory Island and Cape Clear) are reached only by marine warnings, or read Alerts not available.
  • An old answer is hidden, not shown as current. When alerts could not be checked for longer than the source's window, you see Alerts could not be checked recently and no warning sign, although an alert may still be in force. Check the issuer's website in that case.
  • If a MeteoAlarm download fails during a long refresh round (many places on a slow network), the feed can be downloaded once more for every further 120 seconds of that round.
  • The app shows what the issuers publish. For your safety, follow the instructions of your local authorities.
