Free Keys for More Stations

Five national weather services publish their station readings only to registered users. Registration is free, and with your key the app reads their stations like any other network: tested against their neighbours and blended into "now" and into the learned local corrections. A key only matters if you have places in (or near) that country.

Adding a key

  1. Get the key with the steps for its service below (each service's steps are also in Settings, under How to get a free …).
  2. Open Settings › Stations (or Neginato Mezora › Stations & Keys…) and find the network under Networks That Need Your Free Key.
  3. Paste the key into the field and click Save. Spaces and line breaks at the ends are removed.
  4. The next refresh uses it, and the status line shows the result: Working: … (green), or why not (see When a key does not work).

Keys are stored in your login Keychain as "Neginato Mezora: network key", never in a file, and each is sent only to its own service, over HTTPS. Replace… enters a new key; Remove deletes it from the Keychain. The network's switch turns it off and keeps the key.

Met Office (United Kingdom)

  1. Open the Met Office Weather DataHub and choose Login/Register. Register with your e-mail address and confirm it.
  2. In the profile menu, choose My subscriptions › Subscribe, pick the Land Observations product, select its free plan and click Subscribe.
  3. Copy the API key that is shown. It is shown only once; if you lose it, use Manage › Reset Credentials to get a new one.
  4. Paste the key in Settings › Stations › Met Office and click Save.
  • You get: hourly reports of about 150 UK land stations: temperature, humidity, wind and gusts, and the Met Office weather type (which also gives the condition and cloud). The service names stations by area, so a station shows as, for example, "Leeds".
  • Limits: the free plan allows 360 calls a day (reset at 00:00 UTC). The app reads at most the 3 nearest stations of a place, each at most once an hour, and looks up which stations are nearest once a week; places close together share stations. A handful of UK places far apart fit in the limit; with more, the limit can be reached late in the day (orange status), and the stations return after midnight UTC.
  • Credit: "Powered by Met Office data", as the DataHub licence requires.
  • The old Met Office DataPoint service was retired on 1 December 2025. The Met Office also publishes keyless open data on AWS, but it arrives 1–2 hours late, has no gusts, and is licensed CC BY-SA, so the app uses the DataHub instead.

KNMI (Netherlands)

  1. Register at the KNMI Developer Portal with an e-mail address that will keep working, and confirm it.
  2. Log in, open the API Catalog, and on the EDR API card click Request an API key. A key for the Open Data API does not work here.
  3. Copy the key while it is displayed (it starts with eyJ). The catalog later shows only a short hash, which is not the key.
  4. Paste the key in Settings › Stations › KNMI and click Save.
  • You get: 10-minute reports of KNMI's automatic stations, about 50 of which measure temperature: temperature, humidity, dew point, wind and gusts, rain, cloud cover and present weather.
  • Limits: free, also for commercial use, and the key does not expire. 1,000 requests an hour; the app makes about 7 an hour per area.
  • Credit: "Source: KNMI", linked to the dataset (CC BY 4.0). KNMI asks apps to make clear that they changed the data; the credits line says the station data are quality-checked and blended.

MET Norway Frost (Norway)

  1. Open the Frost "Request credentials" page and enter your e-mail address.
  2. The next page shows a client ID and a client secret. Only the client ID is needed; keep both safe.
  3. Paste the client ID in Settings › Stations › MET Norway (Frost) and click Save. Do not paste the secret.
  • You get: the latest reports of MET Norway's stations and of partner stations (for example the Norwegian road authority): temperature, humidity, dew point, wind and gusts, and rain. MET Norway's own stations count fully; partner stations count 70%, and their wind 40%, because their siting varies.
  • Limits: free, with no fixed quota; MET Norway asks for no more than about one request a second on average. The app makes at most 2 requests per place every 5 minutes, and keeps the station list for a day.
  • Credit: "Data from The Norwegian Meteorological Institute" (NLOD 2.0 and CC BY 4.0).

Météo-France (France)

  1. On the Météo-France API portal, choose Sign in › Create account. Pick a user name (not your e-mail address), confirm the e-mail you receive, then sign in with the user name.
  2. Open the Package Observations API (DonneesPubliquesPaquetObservation), version 2, and click Subscribe to the API for free.
  3. Only after subscribing: My APIs › Generate Token, choose API Key, and enter a validity in seconds (up to 3 years: 94608000). A key generated before subscribing does not work.
  4. Copy the key, paste it in Settings › Stations › Météo-France and click Save. The row shows the key's expiry date.
  • You get: 6-minute reports of about 2,000 stations across France: temperature, humidity, dew point, wind and gusts, and rain. The main network counts fully; the extended network (partner and secondary stations) 85%, and its wind 60%. These reports have no cloud cover or present weather.
  • Limits: free; 100 requests a minute. The app reads at most one 6-minute package of all of France every 6 minutes (the newest one at least 10 minutes old, so that it is complete), plus the station list once a day.
  • Renewals: the key expires on the date you chose (the app shows it); the subscription must be renewed every 2 years under My APIs › Renew.
  • Credit: "Source: Météo-France" with the date of the data (Licence Ouverte / Etalab 2.0 asks for both), for example Source: Météo-France, data of 25 Oct 14:06 UTC.

AEMET (Spain)

  1. Open AEMET OpenData's API key request page, enter your e-mail address and tick No soy un robot (I am not a robot).
  2. Within 5 days, click the link in the e-mail Petición generación API Key servicio Aemet OpenData.
  3. A second e-mail, Alta en el servicio Aemet OpenData, contains the key. Copy all of it: it is long, and the first or last character is easily missed.
  4. Paste the key in Settings › Stations › AEMET and click Save. The row shows when it expires.
  • You get: hourly reports of about 830 automatic stations across Spain (including the Balearic and Canary Islands): temperature, humidity, dew point, wind and gusts, and rain. AEMET publishes each hour's reports about half an hour after the hour.
  • Limits: free; 40 requests a minute. The app reads all of Spain with at most 2 requests every 15 minutes.
  • Every 3 months: AEMET keys expire 3 months after they are issued, and there is no limit on renewals. Request a new key the same way and use Replace…. Older keys issued without an expiry date are being phased out: since 15 October 2026 AEMET refuses them more and more often, until they stop working altogether.
  • Credit: "Source: AEMET" with the date of the data (AEMET's legal notice asks for both), for example Source: AEMET, data of 25 Oct 14:00 UTC.

When a key does not work

StatusLikely causeWhat to do
The service refused the key (red)A character is missing or extra; the key is for another product; it expired; or the service has not activated it yet.Paste it again with Replace…. Then check the service's specific cause below.
Met Office: refusedThe account is not subscribed to Land Observations, or the credentials were reset.Subscribe to Land Observations (free plan); after a reset, paste the new key.
KNMI: refusedThe key is for the Open Data API (or is the anonymous public key); only an EDR API key works.Request a key on the EDR API card.
Frost: refusedThe client secret was pasted instead of the client ID.Paste the client ID (it looks like xxxxxxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxxxxxxxxxx).
Météo-France: refusedThe key was generated before subscribing to Package Observations v2, it expired, or the subscription lapsed.Subscribe (or renew), then generate a new key and Replace.
AEMET: refused or expiredThe key is older than 3 months, or was issued without an expiry date.Request a new key and Replace.
The service's limit for this key was reached (orange)The free plan's limit (usually the Met Office's 360 calls a day, with many UK places).Nothing: the app tries again at the next refresh; the Met Office count resets at 00:00 UTC.
Could not reach the service / answered with an error (orange)A network problem or a service outage.Nothing: the app keeps the network's readings of the last 90 minutes and tries again.
not needed by your places yet (grey)None of your places is in the network's area.Nothing: the key is used as soon as a place there is refreshed.

The services' own terms apply to your key. The app sends each service only what it needs (your key and the area or stations around your places; see Privacy, Data & Storage).
