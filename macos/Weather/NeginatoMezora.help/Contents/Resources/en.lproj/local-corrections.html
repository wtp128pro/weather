Learned Local Corrections

Forecast models describe the weather on a grid. They miss what makes your spot different: the valley that runs 3 °C colder than the models on clear nights, the city centre that stays warmer, the lake shore, the hillside. These errors are not random: they come back at the same time of day, day after day. Neginato Mezora learns them for each place from the nearby stations and corrects the forecast.

What is learned

  • When: at each refresh, if the station readings around the place are close and fresh enough to trust (their weighted distance within about 25 km and age within 45 minutes; see Live Station Observations).
  • What: the difference between the measured temperature and what the models said for that moment, before any correction.
  • By hour of day: differences are kept separately for each of the 24 hours of the (solar) day, because a place that is too warm at noon may be too cold at dawn. At most one reading per hour of day is taken every 20 minutes.
  • Averaging: the first readings of an hour are averaged equally; later ones update a running average that remembers about the last two weeks, so the correction follows the seasons.
  • Plausibility: a difference above 10 °C (18 °F) is treated as a bad reading and not learned.

How it is applied

  • Every forecast hour's temperature and feels-like are shifted by the correction of its hour of day; the daily highs and lows are recomputed from the shifted hours. So the correction reaches the whole forecast, not just "now".
  • An hour of day is corrected once it has about 3 readings (neighbouring hours count half), at half strength, and at full strength from 6. Because one hour of day can be learned only once per day for about 20 minutes, expect the first corrections after a few days of normal use.
  • Corrections are smoothed with the neighbouring hours and capped at ±4 °C (±7 °F).
  • If a place gets no new readings for more than 30 days (for example you stopped using the app over the summer), its corrections fade out gradually (to about a third after another 30 days), because the season has changed. New readings bring them back to full strength.

Seeing and controlling it

Settings › Stations › Learned Local Corrections shows, for every place, how much has been learned, the correction now, and a bar for every hour of today. From there you can turn the corrections off for all places, or reset one place or all of them.

Good to know

  • Per Mac: the learning is stored on this Mac (local-bias.json, see Privacy, Data & Storage) and survives restarts and updates. Time with the app closed pauses it; it does not erase it.
  • Places without close stations learn nothing and keep the plain blend. A free key for your country's network (Free Keys for More Stations) may add close stations.
  • Only temperatures (and feels-like) are corrected. Rain, wind and clouds are not, and the weather condition (for example rain or snow) is decided before the correction.
  • Removing a place forgets its learning; adding it back starts from zero.
